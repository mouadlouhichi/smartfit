## 2. Brand system (hard constraints — do not deviate)

### Palette — "Axel" theme

| Role | Token | Hex | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Canvas (dominant background) | `background` | `#050404` | The poster lives on near-black. |
| Card / secondary surface | `card` | `#101210` | Graphite; for panels the hero sits on. |
| Boundary lines | `border` | `#23241A` | Olive-tinted, 1px, used sparingly. |
| Primary text | `foreground` | `#EDEBE6` | Warm off-white, never pure `#FFF`. |
| Secondary text | `muted-foreground` | `#C4C1BB` | Warm gray for subtitles/meta. |
| **The one accent** | `volt` | `#8AD200` | Electric green. One purposeful use per poster. |
| Ink on volt | `volt-ink` | `#101010` | **Near-black ink ALWAYS sits on volt — never white text on green.** |
| Volt support tones | `volt-soft` / `volt-dim` | `#B4E761` / `#699E00` | Supporting steps only, no extra hues. |
| Premium highlight | gold | `#87764D` | **Reserved for Pro/premium surfaces only.** Never decorative. |

A controlled palette: dominant near-black background, warm-gray text, one volt
accent. Do not introduce random colors, second accents, or gradients that
invent new hues.

### Typography

- **Display + body:** Helvetica grotesque (`Helvetica Neue`, system sans) —
  tight kerning, heavy weights for display, confident scale jumps.
- **Mono eyebrows/meta:** JetBrains Mono, small, with the brand pattern of a
  leading dash — e.g. `— FREE · PRIVATE · NO WEARABLE REQUIRED`. Use mono for
  data/terminal accents (stats, reps, dates), never for headlines.
- **Header pattern:** volt (or mono) eyebrow → huge display title → warm-gray
  subtitle. This is the SmartFit editorial voice; reuse it.
- Avoid generic AI typography, excessive fonts (2 families max), unnecessary
  effects, and decorative text.

### Brand mark

- One mark: the **SmartFit flame** (Lucide `flame` glyph, 2-unit round stroke,
  near-black stroke on a volt tile, or volt stroke on near-black). Small,
  placed with intent — it signs the poster, it doesn't decorate it.

## 3. Art direction

First identify the core message and strongest visual asset (a physique in
motion, a training stat, the app UI, the flame, a number like "100+ exercises"
or "0 wearables"). Then build the entire composition around ONE dominant hero
element.

Create a powerful visual hierarchy:

**1. HERO → 2. SUPPORTING ELEMENTS → 3. INFORMATION**

Make the first thing the viewer sees extremely obvious. Use intentional
whitespace (darkspace), strong scale contrast, asymmetrical balance,
sophisticated typography, controlled spacing, visual rhythm, and clear
alignment. Let empty space breathe instead of filling every area — the
near-black canvas IS the whitespace.

Treat typography as a major visual element: the display title can carry the
whole poster. Athletic scale, editorial discipline.

Remove all unnecessary AI-slop: random objects, excessive gradients, fake 3D
elements, unnecessary lines, particles, glows, borders, badges, icons,
clutter, stock-photo gym clichés, and template-like decorations. The only
"glow" allowed is volt doing real work.

Introduce ONE memorable creative idea — unexpected cropping of an athlete,
dramatic scale, typography interacting with imagery (text behind the lifter's
arc, stats set in mono like training data), clever negative space, the flame
mark as oversized crop, striking asymmetry — whatever fits the purpose. One
idea, executed perfectly.

---

## 4. Quality bar

The final poster should feel:

**BOLD — MINIMAL — PREMIUM — CONTEMPORARY — INTENTIONAL — MEMORABLE**

It must look like it was designed by a top-tier human graphic designer for
SmartFit, not generated from a template, and it must sit comfortably next to
the app's landing page and product UI.

Optimize for instant recognition and scroll-stopping impact while maintaining
sophistication. Do not simply improve the original: deconstruct it, remove the
noise, find the strongest idea, and completely redesign it.

**Final test:** If everything except the hero element and primary message were
removed, would the poster's idea still be immediately understood — and would
it still read as SmartFit (dark, volt, editorial)? If not, strengthen the
hierarchy before finalizing.
